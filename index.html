Everything Runs on a Wire

The lines, fiber and radios that move power and data — how they are built, and what keeps them standing.

What We Mean by Infrastructure

Between a generator and a light switch, or a data centre and a phone, sits a chain of conductors, transformers, splices and antennas. Each link has its own engineering: voltage class, span length, loss budget, bend radius, line of sight. Wire High is about that chain — the physical layer everything digital quietly depends on, and the discipline required to design, install and maintain it.

Core Disciplines

Transmission Lines

High-voltage overhead lines move bulk power across long distances at 69 kV and above. Tower geometry, conductor selection, sag and tension, and clearance to ground all follow from the voltage and the span.

Distribution & Substations

Substations step voltage down and split it out; distribution feeders carry it the last few miles. Protection, switching and grounding decide whether a fault becomes a nuisance or an outage.

Read more

Fiber & Broadband

Aerial and underground fiber carries the traffic. Loss budgets, splice quality, slack storage and accurate as-built records are what separate a network that scales from one that is re-engineered every year.

Read more

Structured Cabling

Inside the building, category cable and fiber backbone run to patch panels on a documented plan. Pathways, bend radius, separation from power and certified test results make the difference.

Wireless & Antenna Sites

Towers, rooftops and small cells need backhaul, power and a clear path. Mount analysis, grounding, lightning protection and RF exposure limits govern the work at height.

Reference: Voltage & Media Classes

A rough orientation to the classes of plant discussed on this site. Exact limits vary by jurisdiction, utility standard and applicable code — always confirm against the governing spec.

Class Typical range Where it appears Dominant concern
Extra high voltage 345–765 kV AC Interregional transmission corridors Corona, clearances, right-of-way
High voltage 69–230 kV AC Transmission into load centres Sag and tension, structure loading
Medium voltage 4–35 kV AC Distribution feeders, laterals Protection coordination, reliability
Low voltage 120–600 V AC Services, panels, building branch circuits Bonding, grounding, arc flash
Single-mode fiber 9 µm core, 1310/1550 nm Long-haul, metro, FTTx feeder Total loss budget, splice quality
Multimode fiber OM3–OM5, 850 nm Data centre and campus backbone Modal bandwidth, reach limits
Balanced twisted pair Cat 5e–Cat 6A Horizontal runs to work areas Alien crosstalk, 100 m channel limit
Licensed wireless 600 MHz–39 GHz Macro towers, small cells, microwave Path clearance, interference, RF exposure

How a Project Runs

Survey and load the route before anything is ordered. Design to the governing standard, then confirm the constructability with the crew who will build it. Test every span, splice and channel against a written acceptance criterion, and hand over as-builts that match what is actually in the ground. Maintenance starts the day the system is energised, not the day it fails.
