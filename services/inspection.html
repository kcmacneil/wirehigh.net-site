Wires, structures and hardware degrade in predictable ways. Inspection programmes exist to convert that degradation into scheduled work, and the value of a programme is decided by how consistently its findings are trended and closed out.

Methods

  • Visual and climbing patrol. Still the baseline for hardware wear, corrosion, cracked insulators and damaged grounds.
  • Aerial and drone survey. High-resolution imagery and LiDAR of the corridor, useful for clearance checks and post-storm assessment.
  • Thermography. Infrared scanning finds hot connections and overloaded equipment before they open a circuit.
  • Optical monitoring. Trending splice loss and OTDR events catches a degrading fiber path while it still carries traffic.

Programme design

  • Cycles. Set inspection intervals by asset class and consequence of failure, not by whatever fits the crew calendar.
  • Condition scoring. Score findings consistently so repairs can be prioritised across a whole system.
  • Vegetation management. Growth rate, not last year's cut line, determines the trim cycle for a corridor.
  • Closeout. An inspection that produces no work order is an expense; track findings to completion.

Signals worth trending

  • Repeat operations on the same protective device — usually an unresolved fault, not bad luck.
  • Rising splice loss or increasing bit-error rate on a specific optical path.
  • Corrosion and foundation movement at structures in wet or high-traffic locations.
