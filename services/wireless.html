A radio site is a structure, a power system, a backhaul circuit and an antenna that needs a clear path. The RF design is only as good as the mount, the grounding and the transport behind it.

Site elements

  • Structure. Self-support towers, monopoles, guyed masts and rooftop frames each need a mount analysis before new loading is added.
  • Antennas and lines. Azimuth, downtilt and line loss determine coverage; sweep tests confirm the line and connector work is sound.
  • Power. Rectifiers, batteries and generators sized for the outage duration the site must ride through.
  • Backhaul. Fiber where it exists, licensed microwave where it does not — with a path profile that clears obstructions and Fresnel zone.

Safety and compliance

  • RF exposure. Sites are evaluated against exposure limits, with signage and access control at boundaries.
  • Grounding and lightning. Ring grounds, bonding at every transition and surge protection on lines entering the shelter.
  • Climbing. Competent-climber rules, rescue plans and 100 percent attachment; work at height is the dominant risk in this trade.
  • Structural and zoning approvals. Loading calculations and permits precede installation, not the other way round.

Commissioning

  • Line sweeps and antenna alignment recorded per sector.
  • Throughput and latency verified over the backhaul under load.
  • Alarm points confirmed end to end into the network operations centre.
