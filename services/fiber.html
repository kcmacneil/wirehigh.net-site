Fiber is simple to specify and unforgiving to install. A network either lands inside its optical loss budget or it does not, and most of the margin is spent at connectors, splices and bends rather than in the glass itself.

The loss budget

  • Fiber attenuation. Roughly 0.35 dB/km at 1310 nm and 0.22 dB/km at 1550 nm for single-mode; distance is rarely the binding constraint in metro builds.
  • Splices and connectors. A good fusion splice costs a few hundredths of a dB; a dirty or damaged connector can cost more than a kilometre of fiber.
  • Splitters. Passive optical splits are the dominant loss in a PON design — a 1:32 split alone is about 17 dB.
  • Margin. Leave headroom for future repairs and ageing; a design with zero margin fails on its first cut and re-splice.

Placement

  • Aerial. Lashed to a messenger or built as ADSS, with sag, mid-span clearance and joint-use makeready to sort out first.
  • Underground. Direct-buried armoured cable, or innerduct and conduit with pull tension and bend radius limits observed and recorded.
  • Slack and access. Store slack at splice points and structures so a future repair does not require a new span.
  • Documentation. Fiber counts, buffer tube colours, splice matrices and GIS-accurate routes — as-builts are the deliverable, not an afterthought.

Acceptance testing

  • Bidirectional OTDR traces to locate events and confirm splice quality along the route.
  • End-to-end insertion loss with a light source and power meter, compared against the design budget.
  • Connector end-face inspection before every mating — inspect, clean, inspect again.
