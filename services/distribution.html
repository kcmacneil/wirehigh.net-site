Substations are where voltage classes meet. Distribution is what leaves them: feeders, laterals and services that reach the meter. Reliability here is mostly a question of how a fault is detected, isolated and restored.

Substation building blocks

  • Power transformers. Set the voltage ratio and the fault duty of everything downstream; cooling class and loading limits dictate real capacity.
  • Switchgear and breakers. Provide the interrupting capability, isolation points and the switching flexibility to reconfigure around a fault.
  • Protection and control. Relays measure current, voltage and frequency and trip within cycles; coordination curves decide which device operates first.
  • Grounding grid. A designed grid keeps step and touch potentials safe during a ground fault, and gives surge current a low-impedance path.

Feeder practice

  • Coordination. Fuses, reclosers and relays are graded so the device closest to the fault clears it and the rest of the feeder stays up.
  • Sectionalising. Tie points and normally open switches let crews reroute load while a damaged section is repaired.
  • Voltage regulation. Regulators, capacitor banks and, increasingly, inverter-based resources hold voltage inside limits across the whole feeder.
  • Distributed generation. Rooftop solar and storage reverse power flow at times, which changes protection assumptions and requires anti-islanding controls.

Operational notes

  • Record every switching operation — an inaccurate diagram is a safety hazard, not just a paperwork problem.
  • Track outage cause codes; the top three causes usually account for most of the customer-minutes lost.
  • Test grounds and bonds after any structure work; a lifted neutral is invisible until someone is hurt.
